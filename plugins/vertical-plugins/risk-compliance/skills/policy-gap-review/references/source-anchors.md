# Source anchors: policy-gap-review

This file holds the named, dated regulatory and professional-standard sources the policy-gap-review skill anchors to. SKILL.md cites this file by path; the named anchors are not restated inline. Firm-specific anchors (firm-specific severity ladders, named committee sign-offs, firm-specific role titles, firm-specific section numbering) belong in `references/firm-overlay.md`.

`[verify section]` placeholders mark anchors where the precise section reference still needs verification against the current edition of the source. They are deliberate, not omissions. Fabricating a section reference is worse than leaving the placeholder.

## Enterprise risk and internal-control framework foundation

### COSO Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance (June 2017)

Anchor: The five components and twenty principles of the 2017 ERM Framework. The skill leans on the Governance and Culture component (Principles 1-5: exercise board oversight, establish operating structures, define desired culture, demonstrate commitment to core values, attract develop and retain capable individuals) and the Strategy and Objective-Setting component (Principles 6-9) when the policy in scope is an enterprise-level risk-governance policy. [verify principle numbering against the current Framework edition.]

What this skill relies on: the ERM 2017 expectation that policy frameworks are themselves a control-environment construct, that risk-appetite-anchored policies are rooted in named strategic objectives, and that the matrix can carry coverage views by ERM principle when the firm anchors its enterprise risk policy on COSO ERM 2017.

Link: https://www.coso.org/enterprise-risk-management

### COSO Internal Control – Integrated Framework (May 2013)

Anchor: The five components (Control Environment, Risk Assessment, Control Activities, Information and Communication, Monitoring Activities) and the seventeen underlying principles. Used as a parallel reference for SOX-anchored firms whose policy frameworks anchor on the 2013 internal-control framework rather than the 2017 ERM framework. [verify principle numbering against the current Framework edition.]

What this skill relies on: that policy expectations under the 2013 internal-control framework can be benchmarked against the 17 principles where the firm anchors there, and that gap rows on policy completeness can cite the principle the policy fails to operationalise.

Link: https://www.coso.org/internal-control

## Policy-framework supervisory expectations

### FFIEC IT Examination Handbook — Management booklet (June 2015)

Anchor: §II.A (governance and policy framework expectations), §II.B (strategic planning and policy alignment). [verify section labels against the currently posted edition; the FFIEC has refreshed the Management booklet since 2015 in some chapters.]

What this skill relies on: the FFIEC supervisory framing that the firm's policy framework is itself an examinable artefact, that policies are board-approved (or board-attested) at the level the firm's framework requires, that policy versioning and review cadence are documented, and that the policy framework links to the firm's risk-appetite statement.

Link: https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/it-booklets/management/

### CFPB Compliance Management System guidance — Supervision and Examination Manual (current edition)

Anchor: The CMS chapter framing the four CMS pillars: board and management oversight, compliance program (policies and procedures, training, monitoring and corrective action), consumer complaint response, and compliance audit. The policies-and-procedures pillar is the operative section for this skill. [verify chapter and section labels against the current CFPB Supervision and Examination Manual edition.]

What this skill relies on: the CFPB expectation that policies are tailored to the institution's products, channels, and customer profile (not boilerplate), are reviewed at a documented cadence (typically annual or biennial), and are operationally connected to the training, monitoring, and audit pillars rather than stranded on a shelf.

Link: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervision-examinations/

### OCC Bulletin 2014-39 — Risk Governance for Banks

Anchor: Heightened standards governance expectations for covered banks under 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D, including the policy-framework expectations on the risk governance framework (RGF), the risk-appetite statement (RAS), and the strategic plan. [verify section labels; the bulletin uses numbered sections.]

What this skill relies on: the OCC supervisory expectation that policies in covered banks are tied to the RGF and RAS, that policy-level breaches of the RAS are treated as findings, and that policy refreshes are board-approved at the cadence the standards require.

Link: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2014/bulletin-2014-39.html

## Risk-data and traceability

### BCBS 239 — Principles for Effective Risk Data Aggregation and Risk Reporting (Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, January 2013)

Anchor: Principles 1 (governance) and 2 (data architecture and IT infrastructure) for risk-data policy expectations; Principle 11 (distribution) for distribution-policy expectations. [verify principle numbering against the BCBS publication.]

What this skill relies on: BCBS 239's traceability expectation. A policy that imposes a control must trace to the principle or rule that gives the control its mandate, and a gap matrix that asserts a benchmark requirement traces to the BCBS source rather than to a policy gloss of it.

Link: https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs239.htm

## Model-risk policy expectations

### SR 11-7 / OCC Bulletin 2011-12 — Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management (Federal Reserve and OCC, April 4, 2011, superseded April 17, 2026)

Anchor: §VI (governance, policies, and controls). The section set the policy expectations for the firm's model-risk-management framework: policy approval at the level the firm's framework requires, model inventory expectations, role definitions for development / validation / use, and segregation expectations. Superseded by the joint April 2026 guidance below; retained as historical anchor for policies that pre-date the refresh, where the matrix's `outdated` classification fires on policies still anchored only to SR 11-7.

Link: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm

### Joint Interagency Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management — OCC Bulletin 2026-13 / FRB SR 26-2 / FDIC FIL-15-2026 (April 17, 2026)

Anchor: Section II (scope and definitions), Section III (control environment, third-party model controls). The April 2026 joint guidance supersedes SR 11-7 (2011) and SR 21-8 (BSA/AML model risk, 2021). Most relevant to banking organizations with total assets over $30 billion; non-binding (does not set forth enforceable standards). Generative AI and agentic AI are explicitly out of scope (footnote 3): "Generative AI and agentic AI models are novel and rapidly evolving. As such, they are not within the scope of this guidance... However, the principles described in this guidance apply to traditional statistical and quantitative models and non-generative, non-agentic AI models." The bulletin contains zero references to NIST AI RMF, NIST AI 600-1, or any other NIST publication.

What this skill relies on: the refreshed model-risk policy expectations for traditional statistical and quantitative models and non-generative, non-agentic AI models. The matrix's `outdated` classification fires on MRM policies anchored only to SR 11-7 with no acknowledgement of the 2026-13 refresh, where the policy in scope governs traditional models. For GenAI, agentic AI, or other AI-system policy scope, the matrix benchmarks against NIST AI RMF 1.0, NIST AI 600-1 (Generative AI Profile), ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, and the firm's own AI governance framework rather than this bulletin; gaps in those AI-policy domains route to a separate workstream.

Link: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-13.html (parallels: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm, https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2026/agencies-revise-interagency-model-risk-management-guidance)

## Third-party-risk policy expectations

### Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (OCC / FRB / FDIC, June 6, 2023)

Anchor: §III.A (planning), §III.B (due diligence and selection), §III.C (contract negotiation), §III.D (ongoing monitoring), §III.E (termination). [verify section labels against the published guidance; the lifecycle phases are named explicitly in the text.]

What this skill relies on: the lifecycle expectations the June 2023 guidance imposes that pre-2023 vendor policies typically miss (planning-phase criticality assessment as a discrete step, ongoing-monitoring KRIs and not just annual review, exit planning with periodic testing, subcontractor oversight as a named expectation). The matrix's `partial` classification fires most often on TPRM policies that move directly from due-diligence to monitoring without the planning-phase framing.

Link: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2304.htm

## BSA/AML policy expectations

### FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual — BSA/AML Compliance Program section (current edition)

Anchor: The BSA/AML Compliance Program chapter's four-pillar framing (board-approved BSA program, designated BSA officer, training, independent testing) and the Customer Due Diligence chapter (CDD/EDD policy expectations under FinCEN's CDD Rule, 31 CFR §1010.230). [verify chapter and section labels against the currently posted edition.]

What this skill relies on: the manual's explicit expectations on what a BSA/AML policy must address, the policy-level expectations for SAR and CTR programs, OFAC/sanctions screening policy expectations, and the BSA-officer role definition. CDD-Rule beneficial-ownership policy expectations are a frequent gap site for fintech-bank-partnership policies.

Link: https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual

## Consumer-compliance and conduct policy expectations

### CFPB UDAAP Examination Manual (current edition)

Anchor: The UDAAP exam manual's expectations on consumer-facing policies (marketing review, complaint handling, fee disclosure, product approval). [verify chapter and section labels against the current edition.]

What this skill relies on: the UDAAP framing that consumer-facing policies are themselves examinable, that the unfair-deceptive-abusive standard applies at the policy-design level (a policy can be UDAAP-deficient even when individual transactions are facially compliant), and that customer-impact severity ratings reference the UDAAP standard.

Link: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervision-examinations/

## AI-system policy expectations

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (NIST AI 100-1, January 2023) and AI 600-1 Generative AI Profile (July 2024)

Anchor: The Govern function (categories Govern 1.1 through Govern 6.5) for AI-policy expectations; the AI 600-1 profile's GenAI-specific risk subcategories. [verify subcategory numbering against the current Profile edition.]

What this skill relies on: the AI RMF Govern expectation that AI policy addresses accountability, oversight, transparency, and incident-response at a level proportionate to the AI system's risk; the AI 600-1 expectation that GenAI-specific risks (hallucination, prompt injection, training-data provenance, agentic-AI tool-and-action boundary) are addressed in firm policy where the firm uses GenAI in production.

Link: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

### ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system

Anchor: §6 (planning), §7 (support), §8 (operation). [verify section labels; the standard uses ISO management-system clause numbering.]

What this skill relies on: where the firm runs an AI management system anchored on ISO 42001, the matrix can benchmark AI policy expectations against the ISO clauses. Used as a secondary overlay; primary AI-policy benchmark for US firms is NIST AI RMF 1.0.

Link: https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html

## Cybersecurity policy expectations (cross-cutting, when in scope)

### NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500 — Cybersecurity Requirements for Financial Services Companies (effective March 2017; amended November 2023)

Anchor: §500.3 (cybersecurity policy expectations: approval cadence, content elements, board attestation), §500.4 (CISO appointment and reporting), §500.9 (risk assessment cadence). [verify post-November-2023 amendment numbering.]

What this skill relies on: NYDFS Part 500 §500.3's explicit list of cybersecurity-policy content elements, which is the most prescriptive policy-content list in US financial-services regulation. The cyber overlay loads when the policy in scope is a cybersecurity policy or includes a cybersecurity section.

Link: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/cybersecurity

### FFIEC IT Examination Handbook — Information Security booklet (September 2016)

Anchor: §II.A (governance and policy framework), §II.C.7 (logical security policy expectations), §III (risk identification and assessment). [verify section labels against the currently posted edition; the FFIEC has issued booklet updates since 2016.]

What this skill relies on: that cybersecurity policies in matrices for FFIEC-supervised institutions carry the IT Booklet framing for governance and logical security, parallel to NYDFS Part 500 for institutions in both supervisory perimeters.

Link: https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/it-booklets/information-security/

### SEC Reg S-K Item 106 — Cybersecurity disclosure (effective December 2023)

Anchor: 17 CFR §229.106. Disclosure expectations for public registrants on cybersecurity risk management, strategy, and governance. [verify section labels.]

What this skill relies on: Reg S-K Item 106(c)'s expectation that public registrants disclose how cybersecurity risk management is integrated into the overall risk management system, board oversight, and management's role. The expectation creates a policy-disclosure surface; the cyber overlay flags when the policy in scope is a public-registrant disclosure-process policy.

Link: https://www.sec.gov/

## Privacy policy expectations (cross-cutting, when in scope)

### GLBA Safeguards Rule — 16 CFR Part 314 (FTC, amended 2021/2023)

Anchor: 16 CFR §314.4 on the elements of an information security program; §314.4(a)-(i) lists the program elements. [verify section labels against the post-amendment edition; the 2021 amendment added specific control elements including encryption and MFA.]

What this skill relies on: the Safeguards Rule's prescriptive list of program elements that a privacy or data-handling policy at a covered FTC-regulated entity must address. Bank-supervised institutions reference the equivalent interagency Safeguards rule under their prudential supervisor.

Link: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/safeguards-rule

### Reg P — 12 CFR Part 1016 (CFPB) and Reg S-P — 17 CFR §248.30 (SEC)

Anchor: Reg P consumer-financial-information privacy notice requirements; Reg S-P customer-information safeguards (post-2024 amendment incident-response and customer-notification timing). [verify section labels for the post-2024 Reg S-P amendment.]

What this skill relies on: that consumer-privacy and customer-information policies at CFPB-supervised and SEC-registered entities benchmark against Reg P and Reg S-P respectively, and that the 2024 Reg S-P amendment introduced incident-notification policy expectations that pre-2024 policies typically miss.

Link: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1016/
