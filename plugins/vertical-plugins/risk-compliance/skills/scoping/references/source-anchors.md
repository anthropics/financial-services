# Source anchors: scoping

This file holds the named, dated regulatory and professional-standard sources the scoping skill anchors to. SKILL.md cites this file by path; the named anchors are not restated inline. Firm-specific anchors belong in `references/firm-overlay.md`.

`[verify section]` placeholders mark anchors where the precise section reference still needs verification against the current edition of the source. They are deliberate, not omissions.

## Internal-audit and engagement-planning standards

### IIA International Professional Practices Framework, Global Internal Audit Standards (effective January 2025)

Anchor: Standards covering engagement planning and the internal-audit charter. Standard 9.1 (Internal Audit Charter), Standard 13 (Planning Engagements), and the related conformance requirements on engagement objectives, scope, and resources. [verify section labels under the current 2025 IIA standard set; superseded the prior 2010/2200/2210/2220/2240 numbering used in the 2017 IPPF.]

What this skill relies on: the requirement that every engagement has documented objectives, scope, criteria, and resources before fieldwork begins, and that those are agreed with the engagement sponsor.

Link: https://www.theiia.org/en/standards/

### AICPA AU-C 300, Planning an Audit (clarified standard)

Anchor: AU-C 300 establishes that audit engagements are planned with an overall strategy and a written audit plan. [verify exact paragraph references — .07 through .12 in the current edition.]

What this skill relies on: the discipline that scope is documented before substantive work, and that scope is revisited as facts change. Scoping is the second-line analogue of an AU-C 300 plan, not an audit plan itself.

Link: https://us.aicpa.org/research/standards/auditattest/clarifiedsas

## Enterprise risk and engagement risk

### COSO Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017)

Anchor: Component 3 (Performance), Principles 10-13 on identifying, assessing, prioritizing, and responding to risk in the context of objectives. [verify principle numbering in the current edition; COSO has issued supplemental guidance since 2017.]

What this skill relies on: that scope-setting is part of risk identification and assessment for the engagement itself, not just for the entity under review. An advisory engagement carries engagement risk; an internal review carries program risk. Both are scoped consciously.

Link: https://www.coso.org/erm-framework

## Model risk and prudential governance scope

### SR 11-7 / OCC Bulletin 2011-12 — Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management

Anchor: Section I-II framing on the scope of model risk management — "what counts as a model" and which functions sit in second-line model risk. [verify section labels; OCC Bulletin 2026-13 has revised the interagency model risk guidance.]

What this skill relies on: that model-validation and model-risk-review engagements have explicit scope as to which models, business units, and risk types are covered. Scoping captures that scope formally.

Link: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm

### OCC Bulletin 2026-13 — Revised Interagency Model Risk Management Guidance (2026)

Anchor: Section III on governance scope — board and senior-management responsibility for the model risk program. [verify section labels.]

What this skill relies on: persona role and review-gate definitions for model-risk engagements. CRO, head of model risk, and AI governance lead each have different review machinery under the revised guidance.

Link: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/

### 12 CFR Part 30, Appendix D — OCC Heightened Standards for large insured national banks

Anchor: Part 30 Appendix D, Standards I-V on risk governance framework and three-lines-of-defense. [verify the exact appendix and section labels; the standards address large-bank risk governance.]

What this skill relies on: that large-bank engagements set the right scope for risk-governance reviews, including independent risk management responsibilities, board oversight, and limits framework reviews.

Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-I/part-30/appendix-Appendix%20D%20to%20Part%2030

## Third-party risk scope

### Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (June 6, 2023, OCC / FRB / FDIC)

Anchor: Section II.A on risk-based scope of third-party risk management activity. [verify section labels under the published guidance.]

What this skill relies on: that TPRM engagements explicitly scope which third-party tiers and which lifecycle phases are covered. Scoping consumes the institution profile to set the regulator constellation that drives TPRM expectations.

Link: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2304.htm

### FFIEC IT Examination Handbook — Audit Booklet

Anchor: "Audit Planning and Engagement" section on engagement scoping for IT-audit work; current edition. [verify section labels in the edition currently posted.]

What this skill relies on: scope documentation expectations for IT-audit and IT-risk engagements that touch ICT and operational resilience.

Link: https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/

## Examination scope conventions

### OCC Comptroller's Handbook on examination scope

Anchor: Bank Supervision Process booklet — examination scope-setting process for OCC supervisory examinations; current edition. [verify booklet name and section labels.]

What this skill relies on: that exam-readiness-sprint engagements anticipate the regulator's own scope-setting machinery so the engagement charter aligns to expected exam scope.

Link: https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/

### FRB SR 12-17 — Consolidated Supervision Framework for Large Financial Institutions

Anchor: SR 12-17 on the scope of FRB consolidated supervision for large financial institutions. [verify section labels.]

What this skill relies on: that engagements at FRB-supervised holding companies and large IHCs identify the consolidated-supervisor relationship explicitly in the institution profile.

Link: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1217.htm

### SEC Division of Examinations — Examination Priorities (annual)

Anchor: Division of Examinations Priorities document published annually. [verify the current calendar-year edition.]

## Consulting and advisory engagement convention

### Consulting engagement letter and Statement of Work convention

Anchor: There is no single regulator for consulting engagement letters; this anchor reflects practitioner expectation across Big Four, regulatory consulting, and boutique GRC practices that engagements have a written engagement letter and SOW covering scope, deliverables, fees, and assumptions before fieldwork begins.

What this skill relies on: that advisory engagements have a charter reference recorded (`engagement.charter_reference`) tying the structured scope to the contractual document.
