## 1. Executive summary

This document records a systematic QARP (Quality at a Reasonable Price) **long-only** screen across the AI compute infrastructure value chain. From a ~30-name universe spanning semiconductors, semiconductor equipment/memory, grid & electrification, datacenter power & cooling, datacenter real estate, networking, and hyperscale/cloud, eight names pass the QARP filter. They are presented as one-page summaries, ranked in two tiers, with a comparison table, a filtered-out list, and a prioritized research order.

**Headline conclusion.** The cleanest QARP signal sits in the *picks-and-shovels* layer of AI compute — the foundry (TSM), memory equipment (LRCX), grid/electrification (GEV), and contracted nuclear power (CEG) — rather than the crowded AI-chip and AI-software names. One standout is **Lam Research (LRCX)**, where a December 2025 special-dividend prepayment has artificially inflated the trailing P/E to ~77x, masking a ~20x normalized forward multiple on 54% ROE and 30% FCF margins — a verifiable, time-bound misperception with a clear catalyst.

> **Method caveat.** No live fundamentals/screener API was wired into this session. Metrics were sourced via web search across stockanalysis.com, macrotrends.net, finbox.com, gurufocus.com, Trefis, financecharts.com, company IR pages, and sell-side summaries, all dated mid-June 2026. Items marked `[est]` are consensus/estimate-sourced and must be confirmed in diligence. A screen surfaces **candidates, not conclusions** — every name below requires fundamental follow-up before sizing.

## 8. Second-order thematic sweep — picks-and-shovels for each primary

> **Method.** For each of the 8 primary longs, this section maps one step further out along the value chain to the **less-crowded, less-obvious beneficiaries** the market has not fully connected to the theme. Second-order names are *idea starters*, not recommendations — most need their own QARP/quality screen before sizing. Data mid-June 2026; `[est]` = confirm in diligence.

### 8.1 TSM → advanced packaging, ABF substrates, wafers, EDA

**The second-order angle.** Leading-edge AI ramps are increasingly gated **not by lithography but by advanced packaging (CoWoS) and ABF substrate supply** — the hidden rate-limiter on AI chip shipments. As TSMC expands, it pulls through substrate/packaging demand disproportionately; the bottleneck suppliers carry more operating leverage than the foundry itself.

| Candidate | Role | Why second-order |
|---|---|---|
| **Amkor (AMKR)** | Largest US-based OSAT; advanced packaging partner to TSMC | Captures CoWoS/flip-chip volume; US-listed, profitable |
| **Ibiden (4062.JP) / Unimicron (3037.TW)** | ABF substrate duopoly | Every AI substrate requires ABF (Ajinomoto film) — the true bottleneck; ABF substrate market $4.89B (2024) → $5.42B (2025), advanced-IC-substrate TAM $11.37B (2026) → $15.67B (2031) |
| **SUMCO (3436.JP) / GlobalWafers (6488.TW)** | 300mm silicon-wafer duopoly | Levered to leading-edge wafer-start volume |
| **Synopsys (SNPS) / Cadence (CDNS)** | EDA — royalties on every TSMC reference flow | High-ROIC annuity; see also §8.8 (recurring beneficiary) |

**Underappreciated linkage.** The market prices TSMC's revenue; it underprices the *substrate/packaging capacity* that determines how fast AI chips can actually ship.
